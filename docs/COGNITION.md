# Steelbound — Cognition & Self-Narrative

Owner direction (2026-11-01): run the remaining build back-to-back
"until the full who, what, why, where, how and when — self-asking
awareness, meta-comprehension, cognition, reasoning, the self-dialogue,
and the narrative of each day's episode" is generated by the core
itself.

This document defines that layer in Steelbound's own terms. Everything
here sits on top of the existing invariants: the append-only ledger,
the deliberation gate, and the eval suites bind the cognition layer
exactly as they bind everything else.

## 1. Self-questioning (the six questions)

Before any significant action, and on a schedule of its own, the core
interrogates itself with six standing questions, answered only from
its ledger, memory, and eval records — never invented:

- **Who** — which core is acting (identity, version, generation,
  lineage), and on whose authority (owner instruction, scheduler, RSI).
- **What** — what is being done, in one ledgered sentence, before it
  happens.
- **Why** — the goal the action serves, traced to a goal, queue item,
  or owner instruction. An action with no answerable "why" is not
  taken; the gap itself is ledgered.
- **Where** — where it acts: which data directory, sandbox, tool, or
  endpoint; local-first means the default answer is "on this machine".
- **How** — the method and its risk class under the gate, with the
  approval state recorded.
- **When** — when it acts and when it will report; scheduled work
  names its schedule.

The six answers form a `SELF_QUERY` ledger entry per cycle. This is
the core's operational self-awareness: not a claim about inner
experience, but a verifiable, always-current account of itself that
anyone (owner first) can audit.

## 2. Meta-comprehension & metacognition

- The core tracks what it knows about its own knowing: per-capability
  eval scores, confidence calibration (stated confidence vs measured
  outcome), and a list of known unknowns — questions it has been asked
  that it could not ground in memory, ledger, or tools.
- Comprehension checks: after consolidating memory or adopting an RSI
  proposal, the core re-derives the affected facts from source and
  compares; mismatches are ledgered as `COMPREHENSION_MISMATCH` and
  queued as RSI observation input.

## 3. Reasoning & self-dialogue

- Significant decisions produce a short deliberation record: options
  considered, the evidence for each, the chosen option, and the
  deciding reason — stored as the core's self-dialogue, ledgered by
  hash like a chat turn. The gate reads this record for
  external/destructive actions: no deliberation record, no approval
  request.
- The dialogue is with itself across its own layers (memory proposes,
  evals object, gate decides) — argument in the open, on the record,
  not hidden chain-of-thought.

## 4. The daily episode

Once a day, the core composes the narrative episode of its day from
its own records: what it did (ledger), what it learned (memory
changes), what it improved or proposed (RSI/offspring), what it
refused and why (gate denials), and what it will do tomorrow (queue +
scheduler). The episode is:

- written in first person by the core, from records, with every claim
  traceable to a ledger entry;
- stored as a dated document and an episodic memory item, and surfaced
  in the Core App;
- never embellished: a quiet day gets a short episode.

## Build placement

Two queue items after Offspring cores, before the GUI shell:
"Cognition core" (six-question self-query, metacognition,
deliberation/self-dialogue records) and "Daily episode" (narrative
generation from the day's records, app surfacing).
